# SuperBaitik

## What it is

SuperBaitik is a period and cycle diary for people who want to record period dates and daily observations. It shows a calendar, period and cycle lengths, and an estimated next period start based on recorded dates.

## Launch and onboarding

A cold launch opens directly on the “Today” tab. There is no onboarding flow.

On a first launch, the screen shows “SuperBaitik”, “My cycle”, today’s date, and a card reading “TODAY”, “Your first entry” and “Log the day your period started”. Below it are “Log a period”, “Daily check-in” with “Energy, cramps, and a moment for you”, and “Next period” with “Log two period start dates to see an estimate for your next period.” The screen also offers “Open calendar”, “Guide” and “Privacy policy”, and says “Stored on this device”.

Tap “Log a period” to open “New entry”. Choose “First day”, optionally turn on “Period has ended” and choose “Last day”, optionally add a note, then tap “Save”. “Cancel” closes the entry without saving.

## Screens

### “Today”

- The cycle card shows “Day” followed by the current cycle day after a period is recorded. It says “Period in progress” for an open period or “Since your period started” for a finished one.
- “Log a period” opens “New entry”. While the latest period is ongoing, “Update period” opens that entry for editing.
- “Daily check-in” opens today’s check-in. Once saved, it becomes “Today’s check-in”, with “View or edit your observations”.
- “Next period” shows an estimated date after two start dates have been recorded. “Open calendar” switches to “Calendar”.
- “Guide” opens “Journal guide”. “Privacy policy” opens the policy directly.

### “Calendar”

The screen shows “Your days, at a glance”, a month grid and “Selected day”. “Previous month” and “Next month” change the displayed month; tapping a date selects it. “Today” returns to the current date.

The legend can show “Logged period”, “Open period”, “Estimated start” or “Past estimate”, and “Check-in”. A selected logged day shows “Period · Day” followed by its number, any note, and “Edit period”, which opens the period entry. An open period also says “This period has a start date only. Add its last day when it ends.”

For an unlogged day, “Log a period” opens a new entry using that date. “Daily check-in” opens a new check-in for the selected day; “View check-in” opens an existing one. These controls are available for today and past dates.

### “Journal”

“Daily check-ins” opens the check-in editor and its history. Before any check-ins, it says “Add your first check-in”; afterward it shows the number saved and “View history”.

Under “Periods”, saved entries appear by month, with their dates, any note and “Ongoing” when applicable. Tapping an entry opens “Edit entry”. “Log a period” opens “New entry”; it appears in the empty state before the first entry and in the toolbar afterward.

### “New entry” and “Edit entry”

- “First day” chooses the period start.
- “Period has ended” reveals “Last day”. Both the first and last day count toward period length; a one-day period uses the same date twice.
- “Note (optional)” contains the field “Anything you want to remember?”
- “Save” saves the entry; “Cancel” closes it.
- In “Edit entry”, “Delete entry” opens “Delete this entry?” with “Delete” and “Cancel”. The dialog says “This will remove these dates and your note from the journal.”
- A failed update displays “Couldn’t update your journal” and “OK”, with an explanation.

### “Daily check-in”

“How did you feel?” contains “Day”, “Energy” and “Cramps”. Each field is optional:

- “Energy” offers “Not recorded”, “Low”, “Usual” and “High”.
- “Cramps” offers “Not recorded”, “None”, “Mild”, “Moderate” and “Strong”.
- “Personal note” contains “What stood out today?”

“Save” saves the check-in and closes the screen. “Close” closes it. “Check-in history” lists saved days; tapping one opens it for editing, and the current one is marked “Selected”. An existing check-in offers “Delete this check-in”, followed by “Delete this day’s check-in?”, “Delete check-in” and “Cancel”.

Changing days or closing with unsaved changes presents “Discard your unsaved changes?” with “Discard changes” and “Cancel”. A failed update displays “Couldn’t update check-in” and “OK”, with an explanation.

### “Insights”

“Understand your rhythm” offers the “Measure” choices “Cycle length” and “Period length”.

With enough records, the selected measure shows an average, the number of records, a shortest and longest comparison when applicable, and a chart under “Recent cycles” or “Recent periods”. The display includes up to six recent records. Tapping a cycle interval opens “Cycle recap”; period rows are informational.

When there are not enough records, the screen explains what to add and may offer “Log a period” or “Update period”.

### “Cycle recap”

The recap shows “Cycle dates”, including “First day”, “Next period started”, “Cycle length” and, when recorded, “Period length”. “Check-ins this cycle” shows saved observations and notes with their cycle-day numbers and dates. The navigation back control returns to “Insights”.

### “Journal guide” and “Privacy policy”

“Journal guide” explains how to record a start, finish an entry, check in, read a cycle recap and interpret the estimate. Its “Privacy policy” control opens the policy. “Done” closes the guide or the directly opened policy.

The estimate uses the average of up to six recent gaps between recorded starts, rounded to a whole day and added to the latest start. The guide says: “Use these estimates for personal planning. They cannot determine ovulation or safe days for contraception. Speak with a healthcare professional before making medical decisions.”

## Features

- “Log a period”, including a past start, an optional last day and an optional note.
- “Update period” or delete a recorded entry.
- “Daily check-in” for energy, cramps and a personal note on today or a past day.
- “Check-in history”, with editing and deletion.
- “Calendar” with logged periods, open periods, check-ins and an estimated start.
- “Next period” estimate based on recorded start dates.
- “Cycle length” and “Period length” averages and recent records.
- “Cycle recap” with check-ins placed within a recorded cycle.
- “Journal guide” and “Privacy policy”.

## Behaviours that can look like bugs

- The first calendar says “Choose the day your period started, then tap Log a period.” Select today or an earlier day to make “Log a period” available. A future day says “You can log a period once this day arrives.”
- An open period extends through today until a last day is added. To finish it, open the entry, turn on “Period has ended”, choose “Last day” and tap “Save”.
- Periods cannot overlap. If saving produces “These dates overlap another period. Check the start and end dates.”, adjust the dates or finish the existing open period first.
- Future period dates and check-in dates cannot be recorded. Choose today or an earlier date. A period’s last day must be on or after its first day.
- Check-in “Save” is disabled until at least one energy level, cramp level or nonblank note is entered, and a change has been made. It is also disabled if saved check-ins cannot be opened. For the latter, the app says “Your check-ins could not be opened. Unlock your device and reopen SuperBaitik.”
- “Check-in history” initially says “Your saved check-ins will appear here.” Save a check-in to populate it. Selecting a different day with unsaved changes asks whether to discard them.
- “Next period” needs two start dates. With one start, “Insights” says “You have one start date. Add a previous period from the calendar or record your next period when it begins.” An estimate that has passed remains visible as “Last estimate” until a new start is logged.
- “Period length” needs a finished period. If one is open, it says “When your period ends, add its last day to see the length here.” Use “Update period” to add it.
- A recap without observations says “No check-ins were recorded during this cycle. You can add one by selecting a past day in Calendar.”
- If the diary cannot be opened, the app says “Your diary could not be opened. Unlock your device and reopen the app.” Save failures ask the user to try again.

## Starter content and resume

There is no sample content in the normal app. The journal starts empty.

Saved entries and check-ins remain available for later editing. An ongoing period can be resumed by adding its last day. Unsaved edits are not presented as resumable work.

## Permissions

None.

## Absent

Login or accounts, in-app purchase, ads, analytics, an account deletion flow and an App Tracking Transparency prompt are absent.

User-generated content is present: the person can enter period dates, check-ins and notes.

## Data and support

Entries stay on the device. The policy says they are excluded from backups, are not sent to a developer server or shared with other apps, and remain until deleted. Deleting the app removes the local journal; offloading it keeps its documents and data.

There is no on-screen Contact or Support control.

## Scanning and health

The app does not scan barcodes or QR codes.

It shows menstrual health information entered by the user, calculated cycle and period lengths, and an estimated next period start. The guide explains the calculation and its limitations. No citations are shown.

## Platform

The interface uses English and U.S. date formatting, with a Monday-first calendar. Dates follow the device’s time zone; recorded calendar days retain their day when the time zone changes.

The app supports iPhone and iPad and requires iOS 17.0 or later. iPhone supports portrait and both landscape orientations. iPad also supports upside-down portrait.
